\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why DINOv2 beats DINOv3 here.}
DINOv3 is the newer and larger-scale model, yet it is behind DINOv2 in every configuration we trained. We did not isolate the cause, but one factor is visible in our setup: with patch 16 at $512^2$, DINOv3 matches on a $32\times32$ grid, against $37\times37$ for DINOv2, so its argmax is quantised about $16\%$ more coarsely. Consistent with this, WSA gains more for DINOv3 than for DINOv2 at $\alpha{=}0.05$ ($+4.8$ vs.\ $+4.4$ points on average over the adapted models), and the gap between the two backbones narrows from $5.4$ points (frozen) to $4.4$ (best fine-tuned, both with argmax). A comparison at equal grid size, and with the larger DINOv3 variants that benefit most from its distillation, is left for future work.

\paragraph{SAM.}
SAM's encoder is trained to delineate regions, not to recognise which part of an object a pixel belongs to. Its frozen features are accordingly weak for semantic matching ($22.2$ PCK@0.1), and although supervision more than doubles this, the best SAM model ($47.4$) stays below frozen DINOv2. Adaptation can re-purpose the last blocks, but cannot supply the cross-instance part semantics that DINO's self-distillation learns from far more varied data.

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
For 24 of the 27 training runs, the lowest validation loss is reached after the \emph{first} epoch (after the second for two runs and the sixth for one); training then continues until early stopping, with training loss decreasing and validation loss slowly increasing (supplementary \cref{fig:training_curves}). One pass over SPair-71k is therefore enough to adapt these backbones with our learning rates, and longer training overfits the training images, which are reused across many pairs. This also explains why fine-tuning four blocks is worse than two for DINOv2: more capacity overfits faster, whereas LoRA's rank constraint acts as a regulariser and keeps improving with depth. We select checkpoints on validation \emph{loss}; selecting on validation PCK, as some related work does, could favour a later epoch, since the loss also penalises confident mistakes that PCK ignores.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Each configuration was trained once, so differences below about one point (\eg augmentation, \cref{tab:aug}) should not be over-interpreted. Learning rates, LoRA rank and loss temperature were fixed a priori rather than tuned. We evaluate at $512$--$518^2$, below the $840^2$ common in the literature, which caps the attainable precision at small $\alpha$. Finally, nothing in our pipeline addresses the left/right ambiguity of symmetric parts, which GeoAware-SC~\cite{zhang2024telling} identifies as a major remaining error source.
