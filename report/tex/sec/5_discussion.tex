\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experimental results yield several key insights regarding the capabilities and limitations of Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) in dense semantic matching tasks.

\paragraph{Backbone Comparison: Semantic vs.\ Structural Awareness.}
The DINO family demonstrates superior semantic abstraction, linking object parts accurately even across significant domain shifts. SAM, while exhibiting strong structural and boundary awareness from its mask-based pre-training, is class-agnostic and therefore prone to geometrically precise but semantically incorrect matches.
At zero-shot, DINOv3 achieves the highest PCK@0.10 ($0.306$), followed by DINOv2 ($0.261$) and SAM ($0.209$). After fine-tuning, DINOv3 FT lb1+WSA reaches $0.471$ and DINOv2 FT lb2+WSA reaches $0.470$. The near-parity suggests both architectures saturate at a similar representational ceiling under the training regime considered here.

\paragraph{SAM Fine-Tuning Anomaly.}
A striking finding is that fine-tuning \emph{degrades} SAM below its zero-shot baseline at every adaptation depth: SAM FT lb1+WSA ($0.133$) is $36\%$ below the frozen baseline ($0.209$), and the degradation deepens with more unfrozen blocks (lb4+WSA: $0.113$, $-46\%$). LoRA also underperforms the baseline for SAM (lb1+WSA: $0.157$; lb2+WSA: $0.091$). We attribute this to a pretraining objective mismatch: SAM's image encoder is optimized for class-agnostic mask generation, producing features that carry structural rather than semantic information. Gaussian CE supervision pushes these features toward semantic keypoint localization, but without the semantic hierarchy needed to generalize across object instances. Fine-tuning therefore destroys the structural signal that made frozen SAM useful, while failing to instil semantic abstraction — a form of catastrophic forgetting with no compensating gain.

\paragraph{Depth Collapse for DINO at $N{=}4$.}
Both DINO backbones exhibit a sharp performance collapse when four blocks are unfrozen: DINOv2 FT lb4+WSA drops to $0.337$ ($-13.3$\,pp vs.\ lb2), and DINOv3 FT lb4+WSA to $0.387$ ($-8.4$\,pp vs.\ lb1). Unfreezing too many blocks disrupts the high-level semantic representations encoded in the top layers by pretraining on large-scale datasets, without enough training signal from the relatively small SPair-71k corpus to recover them. The same collapse appears in LoRA: DINOv2 LoRA lb4+WSA ($0.292$) and DINOv3 LoRA lb4+WSA ($0.327$) both underperform their lb2 counterparts significantly, confirming that deeper adaptation — even when parameter-efficient — risks over-specializing the top-level representations.

\paragraph{Inference Refinement via WSA.}
WSA delivers consistent sub-pixel improvements across all configurations, averaging $+0.9$\,pp PCK@0.10. The gain is largest for deeply fine-tuned DINO models (DINOv2/DINOv3 FT lb4: $+2.6$--$2.7$\,pp), where sharper but noisier similarity peaks benefit most from local soft-aggregation. Frozen baselines improve by less than $0.2$\,pp, as their broader similarity distributions already diffuse discretization error. SAM benefits minimally (${\approx}0.2$--$0.4$\,pp), consistent with its lower absolute PCK leaving less headroom for sub-pixel refinement.

\paragraph{Efficiency of Low-Rank Adaptation.}
LoRA adapters (rank 8, $\alpha{=}16$) add $61{,}440$ trainable parameters per block — less than $1\%$ of the ${\approx}7$\,M parameters updated per block in full fine-tuning. Despite this $>100\times$ reduction, DINOv3 LoRA lb2 achieves PCK@0.10 of $0.452$ — $96\%$ of the best full fine-tuning result ($0.471$). Inference throughput is identical to the baseline when LoRA weights are merged, incurring no deployment overhead.

\paragraph{Effect of Photometric Augmentation.}
Photometric augmentation improves PCK@0.10 by $+0.4$\,pp on average. The benefit is largest where overfitting risk is highest: SAM at $N{=}4$ gains $+1.3$\,pp, DINOv2 at $N{=}4$ gains $+0.9$\,pp. One exception: DINOv3 at $N{=}1$ performs marginally better \emph{without} augmentation ($+0.6$\,pp), suggesting that a strongly pretrained backbone adapting only its topmost block can be slightly impaired by photometric distortions. Overall, augmentation is a reliable regularizer and is recommended for all configurations.

\paragraph{Cross-Dataset Generalization.}
DINOv2 and DINOv3 are evaluated on PF-Willow~\cite{ham2016proposal} and PF-Pascal~\cite{ham2017proposal} using SPair-71k checkpoints without retraining (SAM excluded due to OOM). All trends replicate: DINOv3~$>$~DINOv2, $N{=}4$ collapse, and LoRA competitiveness (DINOv3 LoRA lb1+WSA: $0.790$/$0.672$ PFW/PFP, gap~$\leq\!2.7$\,pp vs.\ best FT).

\paragraph{Limitations and Failure Cases.}
Despite the improvements, semantic correspondence remains challenging under extreme conditions. Per-difficulty analysis on the best configuration (DINOv3 FT lb1+WSA) reveals that viewpoint variation causes the largest PCK@0.10 drop ($53.1\%$ on non-variant pairs drops to $40.0\%$ under significant out-of-plane rotation, $-13.1$\,pp). Truncation is the second most harmful factor ($-8.0$\,pp), followed by occlusion ($-5.5$\,pp) and scale variation ($-5.3$\,pp). Symmetry ambiguities (e.g., left vs.\ right eye) remain a persistent failure mode across all configurations.

\paragraph{Training Protocol Constraints.}
All hyperparameters except adaptation depth $N$ were fixed \textit{a priori} (LR, weight decay, LoRA rank/$\alpha$, patience\,=\,7). More principled search was excluded because a single backbone sweep already requires 1--2 days of GPU compute on the hardware described in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, making repeated re-runs infeasible. Fixing all other decisions ensures full reproducibility and fair cross-configuration comparison.
