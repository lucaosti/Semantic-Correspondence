Semantic correspondence is the problem of finding, for a keypoint on an object in a source image, the semantically equivalent point on a different instance of the same category in a target image (\cref{fig:teaser}). Unlike stereo or optical flow, the two images do not show the same physical surface: the matcher must abstract over appearance, pose, scale, occlusion and even rendering domain, while still telling apart parts that look alike, such as a left and a right paw.

Large Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) trained without correspondence labels produce dense features in which semantically similar parts lie close together~\cite{caron2021emerging,oquab2023dinov2,zhang2023tale}. Nearest-neighbour matching on these features is already a strong baseline, and a small amount of supervision on top of them gives the current state of the art~\cite{zhang2024telling}. This raises two practical questions that we answer for three widely used ViT-B backbones, DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2}, DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} and SAM~\cite{kirillov2023segment}: \emph{how good are their frozen features}, and \emph{how much does a light, cheap adaptation buy}?

We follow a four-stage protocol on SPair-71k~\cite{min2019spair}: (i) training-free matching of frozen features with cosine similarity and argmax; (ii) supervised fine-tuning of the last $N$ transformer blocks; (iii) a Windowed Soft-Argmax (WSA) that refines the discrete argmax to sub-patch precision at inference; and (iv) Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)~\cite{hu2022lora} of the same blocks as a parameter-efficient alternative. Every hyperparameter that is chosen from data (the WSA window and temperature, the feature layer, the training checkpoint) is chosen on the validation split; the test split is used once. Our findings are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Frozen last-block DINOv2 features reach $53.2$ PCK@0.1 (per image), DINOv3 $47.8$ and SAM $22.2$, consistent with published numbers and with three independent student implementations of the same project (\cref{sec:comparison}).
    \item Fine-tuning two blocks of DINOv2 raises PCK@0.1 to $83.4$; with WSA it reaches $84.9$, on par with GeoAware-SC~\cite{zhang2024telling}, which uses frozen SD+DINOv2 features at higher resolution.
    \item WSA helps every adapted model, most at the strict threshold ($+3.8$ to $+5.3$ points at $\alpha{=}0.05$ for the DINO models), at no training cost.
    \item LoRA on four blocks recovers $93\%$ of the gain of fine-tuning the same four blocks with $115\times$ fewer trainable parameters; photometric augmentation has no measurable effect.
\end{itemize}
