\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Dense features.}
Each image is resized to a fixed square resolution and encoded by a ViT-B backbone into a grid of patch descriptors $F\in\mathbb{R}^{H_f\times W_f\times C}$, which we L2-normalise along channels. For DINOv2 (patch 14) we use $518\times518$ inputs and a $37\times37$ grid; for DINOv3 (patch 16) $512\times512$ and $32\times32$. In both cases we take the output of the \emph{last} transformer block after the final LayerNorm; \cref{sec:ablations} shows that earlier blocks are much worse. SAM resizes the $512\times512$ input to $1024\times1024$ internally and we use its 256-channel neck output on a $64\times64$ grid. Pixel and grid coordinates are related by the pixel-centre convention $u_f=(u+\tfrac12)\,W_f/W-\tfrac12$, and predictions are mapped back to the original image before PCK is computed.

\paragraph{Training-free matching.}
For a source keypoint $p$ we bilinearly sample its descriptor $d=F^s(p)$ and compute the cosine similarity map $S(q)=\langle d, F^t(q)\rangle$ over all target cells $q$. The prediction is the centre of the best cell, $\hat q=\arg\max_q S(q)$, mapped to pixels.

\paragraph{Window soft-argmax (WSA).}
The argmax is quantised to the patch grid (14--16 pixels at our resolutions) and ignores the shape of the similarity peak. Following~\cite{zhang2024telling}, we keep the argmax as a robust coarse estimate and refine it inside a $w\times w$ window $\mathcal{W}$ centred on it:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde q=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{W}} q\,\frac{\exp(S(q)/\tau)}{\sum_{q'\in\mathcal{W}}\exp(S(q')/\tau)}.
    \label{eq:wsa}
\end{equation}
Cells of the window that fall outside the map get zero weight. Since $S\in[-1,1]$, the temperature $\tau$ must be well below one for the softmax to be peaked. WSA changes only inference; $w$ and $\tau$ are selected per backbone on the validation split (\cref{sec:ablations}).

\paragraph{Training objective.}
For supervised adaptation we turn each similarity map into a distribution over target cells, $P(q)=\mathrm{softmax}_q\big(S(q)/T\big)$, and compare it with a Gaussian centred on the ground-truth target keypoint $q^\star$ in grid coordinates, $G(q)\propto\exp\!\big(-\lVert q-q^\star\rVert^2/2\sigma^2\big)$, normalised over the grid. The loss is the cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}=-\sum_{q} G(q)\,\log P(q),
    \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
averaged over the valid keypoints of a pair and then over the pairs of a batch. We use $\sigma=1$ cell and $T=0.07$: without a temperature the logits are bounded in $[-1,1]$ and the softmax over the $1{,}369$ cells of a DINOv2 grid could put at most about $0.5\%$ of its mass on any cell. The Gaussian target, rather than a one-hot cell, tolerates the sub-cell position of $q^\star$. The same loss is used for both adaptation schemes below.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning.}
We unfreeze the last $N\in\{1,2,4\}$ transformer blocks of the image encoder (7.1\,M parameters per block for all three backbones) and keep the rest frozen, so that the low-level, more generic features are preserved.

\paragraph{Low-Rank Adaptation.}
LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} keeps every pre-trained weight $W_0$ frozen and learns a low-rank update of selected linear layers, $W=W_0+\tfrac{\alpha}{r}BA$ with $A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{\text{in}}}$, $B\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}\times r}$ and $B$ initialised to zero, so training starts from the pre-trained model. We adapt the two MLP layers of each of the last $N\in\{1,2,4\}$ blocks with $r=8$ and $\alpha=16$, i.e.\ $61{,}440$ parameters per block, $115\times$ fewer than fine-tuning the same block. After training, $BA$ can be merged into $W_0$, so inference cost is unchanged.
