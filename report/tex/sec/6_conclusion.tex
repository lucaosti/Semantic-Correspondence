\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This project presented a comprehensive evaluation of Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) for the challenging task of pixel-level semantic correspondence. By systematically analyzing DINOv2, DINOv3, and SAM on the SPair-71k benchmark, we established key insights into the trade-offs between zero-shot semantic abstraction and fine-tuned geometric accuracy.

Our progressive experimental pipeline demonstrated that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{VFMs as Dense Extractors:} Pre-trained vision backbones provide strong zero-shot baselines that differ meaningfully in their balance of semantic versus structural awareness. DINOv3 achieves the highest zero-shot PCK@0.10 ($0.306$), followed by DINOv2 ($0.261$) and SAM ($0.209$), a ranking that persists after adaptation.
    \item \textbf{Inference Refinement is Crucial:} Windowed Soft-Argmax (WSA) consistently improves PCK@0.10 across all configurations by an average of $+0.9$\,pp, with gains up to $+2.7$\,pp on deeply fine-tuned models where feature maps produce sharper, noisier similarity peaks. The optimal window size of $w{=}5$ is robust and generalizes across all backbones.
    \item \textbf{Parameter-Efficient Adaptation:} LoRA applied to the last two MLP blocks ($N{=}2$) achieves PCK@0.10 of $0.452$ on DINOv3 — $96\%$ of the best full fine-tuning result — while training only $122{,}880$ parameters, over $100\times$ fewer than equivalent full block fine-tuning (${\approx}14$\,M parameters).
    \item \textbf{Augmentation as Regularizer:} Photometric augmentation during fine-tuning contributes an average of $+0.4$\,pp PCK@0.10, with the largest gains ($+1.3$\,pp) observed for SAM at greater adaptation depths where overfitting risk is highest.
\end{itemize}

Cross-dataset evaluation on PF-Willow and PF-Pascal confirms that all SPair-71k trends generalize to held-out benchmarks without retraining. While significant improvements were achieved, establishing flawless semantic correspondence under extreme viewpoint variations and symmetry ambiguities remains an open problem. Future research directions may explore diffusion-based geometry-aware features or cross-modal architectures to further bridge the gap between structural and semantic alignment.
